# Career Advice for Transitioning to Industry
*Summarized from an October 2025 PBGG Journal Club by Sabash Dahal, senior data scientist at Bayer and adjunct in UGA's Crop & Soil Sciences*

## Overview
- Industry likes people who can span different domains. Your specific skillset is less crucial than your ability to frame a problem, design a solution, and communicate how it impacts the company
- Industry has a different mindset. The focus is on making products and hitting fast-paced milestones while working in collaborative teams, whereas academia tends to focus on basic knowledge, rigor, and individual effort
- Use internships and co-ops to test industry out. Aim for projects with clear outcomes, and find a mentor you can ask for feedback
- Apply using your personal email in case you lose access to your UGA email before they get back
- Impact statements: For each of your thesis chapters (or projects), distill it down to a 1-sentence summary of its impact, in the form of "This matters because it helps _____ do ______."

## Soft skill development
- Technical skills get you the interview, but soft skills get you the job
- Top soft skills are project management, collaboration/teamwork, business sense (=understanding how the company makes its money), and communication (probably most important)

 ## Networking
 - Networking isn't collecting business cards; it's developing an informal mentoring network
    - Recommended to contact fewer people but more intentionally, and keep a spreadsheet to check back a few weeks later to keep the contact warm. (Even if just a simple "I spotted this article you might like.")
 - Use LinkedIn. Follow companies, and connect with UGA alumni with a personalized request. Many people won't respond, but those that do can be very important for you.
 - Do informational interviews with people whose work you're interested in. Finish by asking if they know 2-3 more people you could talk with.

## Resumes
- A resume is not a CV. Distill it down to 1-2 pages of what is most relevant for this specific job application
- Make sure to include key words from the ad or you won't get past the automated screening software
- You have <30 seconds to convince the reader you're a good candidate, so make it quick and easy for them
  - (Other people have said <10 seconds)
- Focus your summaries on the impact ("Created a new product that allowed X to do Y") and not process ("Studied gene expression in system Z.")
  - Quantify everything you can: Number of dollars, number of data points, % increase, etc. Use numbers to sell your skills
  - Good impact summary is Verb + What + Result [+ Why], e.g., "Improved gene transformation by 55%"

## Interviews
- Come prepared with 6-10 versatile stories you can use for examples of when you succeeded, failed, navigated conflict, and other common situations. (Look up example interview questions online for examples.)
- When talking about your work, be impact-focused: State the problem, 2-3 key results, what changed, and how it's relevant to the company's pipeline
- STAR-L method for answering questions: Situation (the context), Task (your goal), Action (you took), Result (quantified), and Learning (how you grew). Keep to 60-90 seconds or it's easy to start rambling
- Always have some questions to ask in return. Good fallbacks are "What does success look like in the first 6-12 months?", "What would an early win look like?", "What challenges is the team currently facing?", etc.
  - Good to start by asking questions that focus on how to help the company. After that can ask about how company helps you (benefits, salary, etc.).
